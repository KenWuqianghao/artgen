# Research notes — patterns in contemporary art at biennials, 2017–2026

Sources read: Dean Kissick, "The Painted Protest" (Harper's, Dec 2024); ARTnews review of "In Minor Keys" (May 2026); e-flux Criticism on the 61st Biennale; Hyperallergic on the 2026 Whitney Biennial; search summaries of Frieze, Artsy, Sharjah 16, documenta 15, Gwangju 2024.

## Formal patterns (what you actually see)
1. Textiles as politics: tapestries, quilts, patchwork banners with phrases, hand-sewn, hung from ceilings; woven mats; embroidered shrouds. (Barbican "Unravel", Jeffrey Gibson, Yee I-Lann, Amina Saoudi Aït Khay, Mohammed Joha)
2. Ceramics / clay: circles of life-size clay figures (Rose B. Simpson), vessels, clay instruments (Jatiwangi), rotating ceramic disc (Vera Tamari)
3. Earth as material: dirt in a plywood box (Berlin Biennale), bricks with plants (Nkanga, 2026 facade), herbarium + lead-cast bird (Annalee Davis), sand paintings of power grids (Nani Chacon)
4. Archive: vitrines of documents, saved works, Boîte-en-valise, artist-run spaces reconstructed ("The Schools", 2026), removed public-plaza signs (David L. Johnson)
5. Painting is back: naive/folk figurative, murals by collectives, modernist pastiche (Fratino), gouache flora (Wardha Shabbir), batik figures (Wenger), text paintings
6. Found / recycled: lobster traps on a salt rope (Karyn Olivier), radio boxes on pedestals, plaster+mirrors+plushies, detritus tornado on a ceiling screen (Michelle Lopez)
7. Video: the artist films themself doing a ritual / dragging fabric through a forest / dowsing (Kissick's "ethnographic TikTok"); multi-channel; slow
8. Sound: organ pipes and scaffolding maze (Bartolini), glass megaphones with insect sounds + scent, choirs in trees, 8-channel video + oud scent (Sabsabi)
9. Grief and shrines: altars, candles, glass-brick chimney of a burned house (Akashi), wax toddlers (Fraser), bronze processions with wreaths and serving trays (Nick Cave)
10. Costume / mask / garment hanging empty at head height
11. Cast body fragments: glitter casts (Young Joon Kwak), bronze figures

## Discursive patterns (what the wall text says)
- Verbs: invokes, refuses, upends, makes way for, centres, explores tactics of, reactivates, witnesses
- Nouns: relationality (everywhere: Glissant show at CARA, São Paulo, Venice, Whitney), entanglement, kinship, infrastructure, the creole garden, the wake, polycrisis, listening, lower frequencies
- Structure: object → "invokes a memory of X but also of Y" → ends in "free of colonialism and capitalism" / "tactics of refusal"
- Biography does the work: heritage practice (grandmother's weaving) = content
- Kissick's summary: "progressive in content, conservative in form"; "talismans that protect against the present"; meaning is only in the wall text

## Photography pattern (why the first renders looked AI)
Real documentation photos are flat, even, wide, empty, slightly dull (Contemporary Art Daily style). AI defaults to cinematic: single spotlight, silhouettes, fog. Fix: prompt for press-kit documentation, visible exit signs and cables, no people or one person from behind, neutral white balance.
