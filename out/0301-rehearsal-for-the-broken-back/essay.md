What would it mean to take broken back seriously as a method?

In a disused salt warehouse at Dorsoduro, Yusuf Okonkwo-Vieira has installed a long table of photocopied zines and pamphlets, plastic chairs, a kettle and a sign that reads FREE. The materials are USB cables, lead, dried seaweed and clay bricks from a demolished factory. The scale is architectural; the light is a single bare bulb. There is a temptation to walk quickly. The work intimately resists this. Lead gives off a faint smell of the sea.

It is important to say what the work is not. It is not an illustration of labour and the exhausted body, though labour and the exhausted body is everywhere in it. It is not a monument, though it performs the grammar of the monument. And it is not, despite the USB cables, about cables. Yusuf Okonkwo-Vieira stubbornly unsettles the intergenerational economy of the broken back, treating it less as a subject than as a weather. The wall text tells us that the work functions as a talisman that protects against the present. It would be hard to glean this from the work itself, which is perhaps the point.[^1]

To stop here would be to miss the work's quiet insistence. Haraway writes of making kin as that which persists after the event.[^2] *Rehearsal for the Broken Back* takes this seriously. Its sedimented porosity is not a failure of communication but a gift. To stand before it is to be gently reminded that the broken back and non-linear time are not opposites but the same wound seen from two sides.

There is a longer history here. Nixon's account of slow violence reminds us that listening is a political act.[^3] Yusuf Okonkwo-Vieira inherits this thinking but slows it. Where Nixon theorises, the work stages. Where the institution demands legibility, the work offers entanglement: planetary, post-anthropocentric, and unrepentant.

One might object that such wound risks speaking only to those already listening. The objection is fair, and the work knows it. This is why the clay bricks from a demolished factory matters: it is perishable, and it will be returned. Han might call this the achievement subject.[^4] Yusuf Okonkwo-Vieira does not call it anything.

What remains is a question, held open.
