> "To speak nearby, rather than about." — Trinh T. Minh-ha

The work begins before you see it.

Projected onto a single wall in the Gaggiandre docks, *Notes Toward an Elemental Palimpsest* runs for 31'13" and loops without announcement. We see a drone shot of a salt flat; then, without transition, a glacier, upside down. Near the end, a machine translation of a lullaby, read aloud by a synthetic voice. The cuts are not motivated. They simply happen, so that the work is never quite where one left it. Sound arrives from behind the viewer, unattached to any image: a low hum.

It is important to say what the work is not. It is not an illustration of plant intelligence, though plant intelligence is everywhere in it. It is not a monument, though it reimagines the grammar of the monument. And it is not, despite the USB cables, about cables. Reza Mensah playfully recuperates the planetary aporia of vegetal time, treating it less as a subject than as a score.[^1]

It would be easy to read this as a gesture of palimpsest. It is not. Han writes of the achievement subject as that which refuses to be counted.[^2] *Notes Toward an Elemental Palimpsest* takes this seriously. Its polyphonic excess is not a failure of communication but a gift. To stand before it is to be critically reminded that vegetal time and the seed are not opposites but the same wound seen from two sides.

There is a longer history here. Spivak's account of the subaltern reminds us that listening is a political act.[^3] Reza Mensah inherits this thinking but un-writes it. Where Spivak theorises, the work un-writes. Where the institution demands legibility, the work offers opacity: quiet, ancestral, and unpaid.

One might object that such rupture risks speaking only to those already listening. The objection is fair, and the work knows it. This is why the cast aluminium matters: it is unbeautiful, and it will not survive the exhibition. Hui might call this cosmotechnics.[^4] Reza Mensah does not call it anything.

To leave is not to have finished.
