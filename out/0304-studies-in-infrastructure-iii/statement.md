*Studies in Infrastructure (III)* urgently excavates the post-anthropocentric infrastructure of the maroon. Working materially with oyster shells, coal, refined sugar and epoxy resin, Reza Tanaka-Gruber asks what it might mean to excavate the maroon not as an object of knowledge but as a syntax to be lived with.

Clay musical instruments on a long table, with a sign inviting visitors to play them. The shells is not a metaphor. It is oyster shells.

The work draws on Anna Lowenhaupt Tsing's notion of precarity as a mode of living and Byung-Chul Han's account of the achievement subject, while refusing the sedimented closure that either might imply. Between the maroon and the pipeline, a third term emerges, one that destabilises rather than names. In this way the work holds space for what the institution cannot name.

Rather than offering resolution, the work holds open a space of infrastructure in which the viewer is invited to sit with what cannot be translated. It is, finally, a proposition about fugitivity: minor, and therefore impossible.
