*Against Opacity* materially stages the diasporic opacity of the threshold. Working stubbornly with blue tarpaulin, plastic bottles and clay bricks from a demolished factory, Bao Mensah asks what it might mean to stage the threshold not as an object of knowledge but as a situatedness to be lived with.

A tent woven from blue tarpaulin large enough for six people, with cushions inside. The tarpaulin is not a metaphor. It is blue tarpaulin.

The work draws on Donna Haraway's notion of making kin and Karen Barad's account of intra-action, while refusing the durational closure that either might imply. Between the threshold and the civil servant's window, a third term emerges, one that destabilises rather than names. In this way the work upends our grounding in time and space and makes way for imagined futures free of colonialism and capitalism.

Rather than offering resolution, the work holds open a space of opacity in which the viewer is invited to sit with what cannot be translated. It is, finally, a proposition about hospitality and the stranger: unfinished, and ongoing.
