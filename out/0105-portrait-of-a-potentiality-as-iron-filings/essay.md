> "The garden is the smallest parcel of the world and then it is the totality of the world." — Michel Foucault

One does not so much encounter *Portrait of a Potentiality as Iron Filings* as become implicated in it.

Projected onto a single wall in an abandoned cinema near Rialto, *Portrait of a Potentiality as Iron Filings* runs for 7'59" and loops without announcement. We see a choir singing a shipping forecast; then, without transition, an underwater shot of mosquito netting sinking. Near the end, an empty swimming pool at dusk. The cuts are not motivated. They simply happen, so that the work is never quite where one left it. Sound arrives from behind the viewer, unattached to any image: wind over a microphone.

It is important to say what the work is not. It is not an illustration of cosmotechnics, though cosmotechnics is everywhere in it. It is not a monument, though it reactivates the grammar of the monument. And it is not, despite the iron filings, about filings. Ravi Lindqvist patiently holds space for the submerged unbecoming of the loom as computer, treating it less as a subject than as a score.[^1]

To stop here would be to miss the work's quiet insistence. Mignolo writes of epistemic disobedience as that which cannot be reduced to transparency.[^2] *Portrait of a Potentiality as Iron Filings* takes this seriously. Its polyphonic precarity is not a failure of communication but a gift. To stand before it is to be obliquely reminded that the loom as computer and the right not to be understood are not opposites but the same wound seen from two sides.

There is a longer history here. Povinelli's account of geontopower reminds us that the margin is where the centre goes to be forgiven.[^3] Ravi Lindqvist inherits this thinking but softens it. Where Povinelli theorises, the work excavates. Where the institution demands legibility, the work offers porosity: intergenerational, fragmentary, and unhurried.

One might object that such afterlife risks aestheticising suffering. The objection is fair, and the work knows it. This is why the raw unfired clay matters: it is perishable, and it will not survive the exhibition. Fisher might call this hauntology.[^4] Ravi Lindqvist does not call it anything.

To leave is not to have finished.
