*Where Were You When the Mother's Kitchen Ended?* radically queers the subaltern worlding of the mother's kitchen. Working radically with 16mm film transferred to HD, silk organza, cowrie shells and a faint smell of diesel, Ngozi Ionescu asks what it might mean to queer the mother's kitchen not as an object of knowledge but as a relationality to be lived with.

On a single screen, the work moves between hands passing a stone from one person to the next, close-up, a woman reading a list of names to the sea, and text scrolling too fast to read. Nothing is explained.

The work draws on Anna Lowenhaupt Tsing's notion of precarity as a mode of living and Christina Sharpe's account of wake work, while refusing the precarious closure that either might imply. Between the mother's kitchen and the boat, a third term emerges, one that makes visible rather than names. In this way the work refuses that convention in favour of a more personal point of view, one that centres experiences of survival and the land.

Rather than offering resolution, the work holds open a space of worlding in which the viewer is invited to sit with what cannot be counted. It is, finally, a proposition about inherited practice: insistent, and perhaps ongoing.
