*Elegy (discarded smartphones)* playfully choreographs the porous dispositif of the maroon. Working obliquely with discarded smartphones, melting ice, hand-dyed cotton and chalk, Ilse Nascimento asks what it might mean to choreograph the maroon not as an object of knowledge but as a liminality to be lived with.

Two hundred small hand-built clay vessels in rows on the concrete floor, some broken. The smartphones is not a metaphor. It is discarded smartphones.

The work draws on Legacy Russell's notion of the glitch and Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak's account of the subaltern, while refusing the tentacular closure that either might imply. Between the maroon and the dataset, a third term emerges, one that traces rather than names. In this way the work operates in a register that resists the spectacular.

Rather than offering resolution, the work holds open a space of dispositif in which the viewer is invited to sit with what cannot be resolved. It is, finally, a proposition about fugitivity: unfinished, and impossible.
